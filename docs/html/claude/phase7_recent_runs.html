METHODOLOGY CAVEAT — per-iteration wall clock is unusable in these 60 s captures The per-iteration wall clock from these captures is non-monotonic and physically impossible: capture iter-p50 = bs128 769 ms / bs160 389 ms / bs192 341 ms — a bigger batch showing shorter iterations cannot be real throughput. Only per-iteration GPU-kernel durations (less contaminated by the spin-wait artifact) are used below. These are still subject to nsys graph-node tracing noise, stochastic mask shape, and thermal/clock variation — they are not immune, just less contaminated.

Direction matches Measurement A (U-shape, sweet spot at bs160), but Measurement B's bs128 anchor is job 75082 on gpu06 — a cross-node comparison — so treat B as supporting evidence, not an independent replicate.
Likely mechanism — HYPOTHESIS, not proven GEMM wave/tile quantization: the autotuner picks different nvjet_* tile shapes per batch size; bs160 may select a tile (e.g. 256×160) that divides cleanly, while bs192 leaves a partial-wave tail. Note: elementwise_triton grew the most in absolute terms across batches (73.9 → 88.8 → 114.3 ms/iter), so memory-bound elementwise/reduction scaling is a live alternative mechanism. The hypothesis is not proven.

§7.7 Finding 2 — the residual also grows with batch

This CORRECTS an earlier "fixed residual amortization" claim A prior framing assumed the comms residual was roughly batch-independent (fixed model size → fixed gradient all-reduce), so increasing batch would amortize it for free. The data below refutes this: the residual grows from 415 to 506 ms across bs128→192. The correction was caught by a Codex GPT-5.5 high-reasoning review and then verified in the data.

Residual reconstructed as: iter_ms = batch × 8 / img_s from soak throughput (§6.B.7 controlled runs), then subtract Measurement A compute.

Batch Iter ms (soak) Compute ms Residual ms Δ iter Δ compute Δ residual
128 624 209 415 — — —
160 696 251 445 +71 +42 +30
192 817 311 506 +121 +61 +61

For the 160→192 step, the +121 ms iter-time growth splits ~evenly between compute (+61 ms) and residual (+61 ms).

The bf16 gradient all-reduce is batch-independent (fixed model size), so the growing part of the residual must be the f32 loss-side collectives — Sinkhorn over masked tokens (K ∝ batch), plus DINO/iBOT center all-reduces. This is the same target as ladder step 7.4 (Sinkhorn), reached independently via the differential analysis.

§7.7 Conclusion — two effects, ~equal weight

The 160→192 diminishing return in the §6.B.7 soak (wall img/s: +12% for 128→160, then only +2.6% for 160→192, at flat MFU) is explained by two independent mechanisms of approximately equal weight:

  • Per-image GPU compute regression — a U-shaped efficiency curve with sweet spot at bs160 (wave/tile quantization, −4.1% then +3.5%). Compute is not balanced due to a straggler; per-rank compute CV is <1.5% at every batch size. This is not a compute straggler problem.
  • Residual growth — the exposed-NCCL residual grows (+30 ms at 128→160, +61 ms at 160→192), driven by batch-scaling f32 loss-side collectives (Sinkhorn K∝batch), not by the fixed-size gradient all-reduce.

The old "fixed-residual amortization" story (residual is constant → bigger batch always amortizes it) is refuted.

§7.3 Metric-gate result — NOT an MFU win

The metric-gate is NOT a throughput or MFU gain Gating allreduce_metrics to every 10 iters (job 80547, period=10) versus every iter (baseline 75082, period=1) produced the following GPU-side result: the NVTX allreduce_metrics range drops to 0.0 ms on skipped iters (the gate works mechanically) — but the deferred collective bill relocated, it did not vanish. This is NOT an MFU win.
Metric 75082 (period=1, gpu06) 80547 (period=10, gpu02) Verdict
NVTX allreduce_metrics range (skipped iters) 314.5 ms 0.0 ms Gate works mechanically ✓
GPU-side exposed NCCL (nccl class, ms/iter) 787 760 −3.5% — small lever
Exposed NCCL (ms/iter) 729 706 −3.2%
untracked (ms/iter) 37 233 +521% — wait relocated here

The 32.6% NVTX share attributed to allreduce_metrics in 75082 was mostly "the bill coming due" — the deferred DDP gradient all-reduce surfacing at that barrier — not the intrinsic cost of the metrics-specific scalar collective. When the gate removes that barrier on skipped iters, the DDP-grad bill relocates into untracked instead of vanishing. The f32 loss-side Sinkhorn/center bulk that does grow with batch (§7.7 Finding 2) is unaffected by the metric gate.

Implication — redirect to §7.4 (Sinkhorn) Metric-gating is a ~3% GPU-side lever at best. The prime comms target is now 7.4 — Sinkhorn collectives (≥3 blocking fp32 all-reduce/iter, teacher-side), confirmed as the batch-scaling residual driver by both §7.3 and §7.7. NVTX-tag the Sinkhorn and center all-reduce callsites first to confirm shares before touching training math.

Straggler finding — arrival skew, not compute imbalance

Compute is balanced across all ranks: per-rank non-NCCL compute CV is <1.5% at every batch size tested. There is no compute straggler.

However, a consistent collective-arrival straggler was observed in the high ranks:

  • gpu02 runs (jobs 80545/80546/80547): rank 6 reaches collectives late.
  • gpu06 run (job 75082): rank 7 reaches collectives late.
  • Example (bs192): ranks 6/7 NCCL-exposed ~65/107 ms vs ranks 0–5 ~880 ms, at identical ~311 ms compute per rank.

The other ranks spin-wait for the straggler at the collective barrier (~880 ms exposed NCCL in bs192). The delay is host-side (data loading, H2D transfer, NUMA topology), not GPU compute, so GPU-compute load-balancing does not apply. This is orthogonal to the batch-scaling question (§7.7 Finding 2) and worth a separate investigation.
