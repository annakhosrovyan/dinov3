Glossary Reference
AC — Activation Checkpointing
Frees activation tensors during forward pass; recomputes them during backward. Trades FLOPs for HBM. Selective AC saves only expensive outputs (matmul, SDPA, reduce-scatter tensors) and recomputes the rest — preferred variant here. Full AC recomputes everything — maximum memory savings.
This project: sel-AC saves 10.6 GB at bs=96 (24.9 → 14.3 GB). Surprisingly, AC is also throughput-positive here — see AC Tradeoff section.
bs — batch_size_per_gpu
Images processed by each of the 8 GPUs per training step. Total effective batch = bs × 8. Config key: train.batch_size_per_gpu.
Safe operating points: bs=64 (conservative), bs=96 (current default), bs=128 (investigational — original OOM unresolved).
CV — Coefficient of Variation
Standard deviation ÷ mean, expressed as %. Measures relative noise in a metric regardless of its scale. Lower CV = more stable, more trustworthy single-run comparisons.
Stage A: A2 (LL128) had CV 4.9% — very stable. Stage C baseline (C1): CV 18% — structured noise, hard to interpret. Rule of thumb: δ < CV is untrustworthy.
DDP — Distributed Data Parallel
Each GPU holds a full copy of the model. Gradients are all-reduced at the end of each backward pass. Simple, no per-layer communication overhead during forward.
FSDP2 is the platform going forward (Tim Darcet, 2026-04-25) — see "Why FSDP2". The archived DDP screening (jobs 9630/9631/9651, 2026-04-03, 100-iter short soaks) reported 18–23 % MFU and ~4 k img/s at bs=128, but the matched May 21 long-soak DDP bs=96 calibration (job 48312, gpu03, 1000 iters) reports ~1,400 img/s · ~8 % MFU with the same MFU formula — only +8.4 % over FSDP2 cell 45367, not 3×. The archived rows therefore are not a defensible benchmark target until the original 100-iter recipe is rerun on today's codebase. See Status dashboard Q5 and the archived-table caveat. Attribution deferred pending same-node FSDP2 reshT noAC A/B on gpu03.
FSDP2 — Fully Sharded Data Parallel v2
Each GPU holds 1/N of each layer's parameters. Before each layer's forward pass, an all-gather reconstructs the full layer. After each layer's backward, a reduce-scatter distributes the gradients. Implements ZeRO-3 semantics when reshard_after_forward=True.
12 transformer blocks → 12 all-gathers (forward) + 12 reduce-scatters (backward) per step. This is why NCCL = 70–80% of kernel time.
H2D — Host-to-Device Transfer
Moving data from CPU RAM to GPU HBM. Potential bottleneck if DataLoader can't feed GPUs fast enough and the transfer isn't overlapped with compute.
In Phase 5 nsys traces: H2D = ~1% of GPU time at both bs=96 and bs=128. Not the bottleneck. DataLoader pipeline (pin_memory, non_blocking, persistent_workers) is already well-tuned.
iBOT — Masked Image Modeling Loss
One of the two self-supervised objectives (alongside DINO). Predicts masked patch tokens using Sinkhorn-Knopp normalization. Uses stochastic per-batch mask shapes.
The dynamic mask shape is why max-autotune and CUDA graphs are incompatible with this codebase — stochastic shapes prevent static graph capture. Only compile_mode=null (default induction-only) works.
LL128 — NCCL Low-Latency Protocol
A NCCL communication protocol using 128-byte aligned double-buffering. Reduces per-chunk overhead for medium-sized collectives. Set via NCCL_PROTO=LL128.
Stage A: +12.1% img/s on gpu07 (300 iters, CV 4.9%). Stage C: same config but +6–7% with CV 18% — likely cluster background load. Not yet shipped. Forcing LL128 beats NCCL's auto-selection for this per-block collective pattern.
MAMF — Maximum Achievable MFU
A tighter practical ceiling than NVIDIA's published FLOPS. Approximately 794.5 TFLOPS for H100 BF16, accounting for memory bandwidth limits and realistic matmul efficiency. NVIDIA's 989 TFLOPS assumes dense (non-sparse) matmuls; 1979 TFLOPS assumes 2:4 sparsity.
~23.9% MFU at the 989-TFLOPS basis ≈ ~29.8% of MAMF. The lab's 30% target refers to the 989-TFLOPS basis (dense MFU).
MFU — Model FLOP Utilization
Fraction of theoretical peak GPU compute actually used during training. Formula: (actual FLOPs/sec) / (hardware peak FLOPs/sec). Higher = more compute-efficient. Does not account for communication overhead.
Current: ~7–8% dense (989 TFLOPS basis). Lab target: 30%. The gap is mostly NCCL overhead eating wall time — the compute kernels themselves are fine.
NCCL — NVIDIA Collective Communications Library
The library that implements distributed operations: all-gather (assemble sharded params), reduce-scatter (distribute gradients), all-reduce (synchronize metrics). Runs as CUDA kernels, visible in nsys traces as ncclDevKernel_*.
Phase 5 profiler finding: NCCL = 70–80% of kernel time at 8-GPU. NCCL↔compute overlap = only 11–16%. This is the dominant bottleneck for Phase 5.
NORS — AVOID_RECORD_STREAMS
TORCH_NCCL_AVOID_RECORD_STREAMS=1. Cuts stream-record bookkeeping overhead around NCCL kernel launches. Reduces lifecycle tracking for NCCL buffers.
Stage A alone: +5.7% (A5). Stacks badly with LL128 (Stage B B1 combined: −6%). Parked from production defaults — don't use with LL128.
reshT / reshF — reshard_after_forward
reshard_after_forward=True (reshT, default): parameters are re-sharded after each layer's forward. This is ZeRO-3 — minimal resident memory, maximum communication. reshard_after_forward=False (reshF, "no-release"): parameters stay unsharded through the full forward pass — closer to DDP memory layout.
At bs=96, reshF without AC is the worst cell (1,143 img/s vs reshT sel 1,351); reshF with AC is roughly tied with reshT — FSDP2 prefetch already hides the reshT all-gather cost. At bs=128, a possible story flip is being investigated (2026-05-20, job 47554): reshF + sel AC scored 1,341 mean / 1,312 median img/s on gpu05 vs an older reshT sel anchor on gpu07 = 1,276 (+5.1%). The mechanism (larger per-step compute makes the all-gather-on-forward pattern win again) is plausible, but the comparison crosses both node and date — needs a paired same-node A/B to confirm before promotion.
ZeRO-3 — Zero Redundancy Optimizer Stage 3
Shards parameters, gradients, AND optimizer states across all GPUs. Each GPU holds 1/N of everything. Maximum memory efficiency at the cost of more communication. FSDP2 with reshard_after_forward=True implements ZeRO-3 semantics for the forward/backward pass.
For ViT-B (85M params) at 8-GPU: each GPU holds ~10.6M params during idle. During each layer's forward, the other ~74.4M are gathered then immediately released.
NVLS — NVLink SHARP / NVSwitch
NVSwitch-aware algorithm that performs reduce operations in the switch fabric rather than over the PCIe/NVLink bus. Available on DGX H100 systems. Set via NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE=1 or NCCL_ALGO=NVLS.
Stage A A1: −8.7% with NVLS enabled. The autotuner also didn't select NVLS in the nsys traces despite it being available. Parked — this workload's collective sizes don't land in NVLS's sweet spot.
The 4 Bottleneck Hypotheses Framework

Phase 5 opened with four candidate bottlenecks (A–D), each predicting a different root cause for the ~7–8% MFU. Hypotheses were tested against nsys traces from jobs 39140 (bs=128) and 39141 (bs=96).

A
Multi-crop Sequential Forward
Global crops (seq=197 tokens) and local crops (seq=37 tokens) are processed sequentially in the same block loop. Predicted ≥3× attention kernel bimodality due to the different sequence lengths. If real, packed attention (NestedTensor + FA2 varlen) could give 2.5–3× attention speedup.
Evidence: nsys showed flash_fwd_kernel min/max ratio of only 1.5–1.7×. Not the predicted ≥3× signature.
Demoted — modest effect, not primary
B
NCCL Serialization (all-gather stall)
With 12 transformer blocks and ZeRO-3, each block requires an all-gather before forward and a reduce-scatter after backward. If these dominate step time and can't be overlapped with compute, NCCL becomes the wall-clock bottleneck.
Evidence: NCCL = 70% (bs=128) / 80% (bs=96) of kernel time. NCCL↔compute overlap = 16% / 11%. AllGather alone = 347 sec over a 90-sec trace window (37,024 launches).
✓ Confirmed — dominant bottleneck
C
H2D Not Overlapped
Seven tensors are moved from CPU (DataLoader) to GPU each step via non_blocking=True. If the H2D transfers aren't actually overlapping with NCCL or compute, they could be stalling the main CUDA stream.
Evidence: H2D↔compute overlap = 0% in traces. But H2D total time = ~1% of GPU time at bs=128. Negligible absolute cost.
Real but minor — <1% of step time
D
Python GC / Eval / Checkpoint Stalls
Python's garbage collector runs across ranks at different times, creating periodic per-rank stragglers. Eval and checkpoint phases synchronize all ranks, potentially creating long tail events.
Evidence: bs=128 p99 union gap = 0.5 ms (no GC stalls visible). GC is already partially mitigated in train.py (gc.disable() + manual gc.collect() every 150 iters). Straggler rank changes between runs (rank 7 / rank 4) → non-deterministic / nsys-induced.
Not primary — already partially mitigated
Bottom line: Hypothesis B is the one. NCCL all-gathers and reduce-scatters consume 70–80% of every step. Only 11–16% of that time overlaps with useful compute. Fixing this requires either reducing NCCL volume (coarser wrapping, gradient accumulation) or improving overlap — not kernel optimization.

Why does bigger batch hurt at 8-GPU? Larger activations mean more all-gather payload per step. ViT-B (~85M params) is small, so NCCL volume is already large relative to compute at any batch size — scaling batch increases both, but NCCL grows proportionally more. See bs=128 Puzzle section.
AC Tradeoff: The Surprise Mental Model

The textbook AC tradeoff is: save memory by paying FLOPs to recompute activations → throughput goes down slightly. At ViT-B / bs=96 / H100 BF16, the opposite happens.

Why AC is faster here: The dominant cost is activation working-set size, not arithmetic. Keeping 24.9 GB of activations live throughout the forward→backward window creates HBM cache pressure and allocator overhead that slows effective throughput. AC drops them immediately and recomputes — the recompute cost is less than the pressure cost. This is a regime-specific result: we are not compute-bound enough for the textbook tradeoff to hold at H100 BF16 speeds.

Also striking: per-GPU MFU drops from 20–22% (1-GPU) to 7–8% (8-GPU) — each GPU does only ~38% of what it can do solo. Within-run σ drops from ±1 pp (8-GPU) to ±0.05 pp (1-GPU), proving nearly all the noise at 8-GPU comes from cross-rank synchronization scatter (NCCL straggler timing, GC desync, thermal drift), not from intrinsic GPU compute variance.
Conclusion (original 2026-05): The bs=128 deficit at 8-GPU is NCCL/FSDP scheduling overhead, not a kernel or Tensor-Core saturation problem. The compute side wants the bigger batch (+6.7%). MFU Explained Metric
What MFU measures
MFU = (model_FLOPs × images_per_sec) / hardware_peak_FLOPs

FLOPs here are actually MACs (multiply-adds) × 2, following the DINOv2 paper / fvcore convention. A "100% MFU" system uses every available FLOP of hardware compute with no overhead.

MFU does NOT account for communication time — a system at 8% MFU that's 80% communication-bound is not "wasting" 92% of compute; 80% of that is unavoidably spent communicating.

Two ceilings
989 TFLOPS
Dense BF16. Standard MFU reporting basis. NVIDIA published "989" for H100 SXM5. All Phase 5 MFU numbers use this.
794.5 TFLOPS
MAMF (Maximum Achievable MFU) — practical ceiling accounting for memory bandwidth limits and realistic matmul efficiency. ~23.9% at 989 basis ≈ ~29.8% of MAMF.
1979 TFLOPS — DO NOT USE
NVIDIA's advertised number assumes 2:4 structured sparsity. Standard transformer training uses dense matmuls. This figure misleadingly halves reported MFU.
Where we stand

The 22 pp gap between current (8%) and target (30%) is not a compute problem — the 1-GPU numbers show the compute kernels can reach 20–22% on their own. The gap is almost entirely NCCL overhead not being hidden by compute. NCCL knob tuning can close ~1–2 pp. The rest requires architectural changes: fewer collectives per step (gradient accumulation, coarser wrapping) or more compute per collective (larger model, more nodes).
Why FSDP2 (Settled) Decision

This is not an open question. DDP is closed for this project. The rationale comes directly from the DINOv2/v3 team:

"I have not used DDP once since 2022 … In FSDP you have the option to not release shards, in which case it's basically equivalent to DDP, with the gather moved before the fwd instead of after the bwd … I think both are fine if both fit." — Tim Darcet, DINOv2/v3 co-author, 2026-04-25

Translation: FSDP2 with reshard_after_forward=False is mathematically equivalent to DDP (just with the all-gather reordered from post-backward to pre-forward). The screening-time 0.4 pp gap between FSDP2 and DDP+ES at bs=256 was specifically from reshard_after_forward=True (ZeRO-3, more NCCL ops). At bs=96 with selective AC, the reshape×AC matrix shows the reshard flag barely matters once AC is on.

Why not go back to DDP? FSDP2 provides the right memory-safety envelope as model scale or batch size increases. Once NCCL is the dominant bottleneck, DDP and FSDP2 have the same core problem — the difference is just when the collective happens in the step, not how much communication there is.

Operational rule: All Phase 5 experiments use FSDP2 ZeRO-3 (reshard_after_forward=True). FSDP2 remains the scale-up platform of record.
Caveat — updated 2026-05-21 with first measurement. Tim Darcet's equivalence is algorithmic: reshF and DDP do the same collectives. Phase 5 throughput does not bear out a practical equivalence: bs=96 reshF noAC (1,143 img/s) was worse than reshT noAC (1,279). The DDP bs=96 calibration (job 48312, gpu03, 2026-05-21) now provides the first matched-recipe data point: DDP mean 1,387 img/s vs FSDP2 reshT 1,279 (+8.4 %), MFU +0.62 pp, peak alloc +0.9 GB, eval+ckpt did not exceed training peak. Single run, cross-node + cross-date, with a monotonic within-run rise (1,310 → 1,419) that exceeds the reported Δ — read as "DDP observed faster in this run," not "DDP is faster."

Separately: the archived 2026-04-03 DDP screening reported ~4 k img/s · 18–23 % MFU at bs=128 (100-iter soaks). The May 21 long-soak does not reproduce that throughput regime even though the MFU formula and script knobs are unchanged. Until the original 100-iter recipe is rerun on today's code, those archived numbers are convention-mismatched against Phase 5 and should not be cited as a target. The plausible reads (not mutually exclusive) are short-soak measurement convention vs. seven weeks of code/runtime drift; recipe/config drift, dynamic-shape recompiles, and per-rank stragglers are additional confounds we have not ruled out.
