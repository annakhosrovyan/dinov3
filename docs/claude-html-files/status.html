Why AllGather dominates at 69.5%: FSDP2 ZeRO-3 (reshard_after_forward=True) issues one AllGather per transformer block on the forward pass AND on the backward pass — 12 blocks × 2 = 24 AllGathers per step. Missing-Cell Probe — bs=128 × reshF × sel AC job 47554 · gpu05 · 1000 iters · 2026-05-20
Finding (Codex-tightened): The previously-untested cell bs=128 + reshF + sel AC hit mean 1,341 / median 1,312 img/s at iter [500,999], MFU 7.67%, CV 10.6%. The +5.1% delta vs bs=128 reshT (1,276 on gpu07) crosses both node and date, so it is consistent with reshF being the mechanism but does not prove it from one run. Mean is partly spike-inflated (bursts to 1851 and 1680); median is the defensible point. The [100,299] ≈ [500,999] equality (1340 vs 1341) is the strongest internal-validity signal — the run did not drift over 1000 iters. The Stage C "park bs=128" verdict is reopened, not revised to "viable candidate."
What this run does establish:
  • bs=128 reshF sel AC ran 1000 iters cleanly — no late-stage degradation, no OOM, no retries
  • Throughput band is at least competitive with bs=96 reshF cells on other nodes
  • Within-run jitter is in the same CV band as bs=96 1000-iter runs (~10%)
  • Memory peak 18.6 GiB rank-0 in this recipe — well below cap, but eval+ckpt cycles not exercised
What it does NOT establish:
  • That the +5.1% is a reshF effect rather than gpu05-vs-gpu07 noise
  • That bs=128 actually beats bs=96 in production conditions (1,341 vs bs=96 reshT sel = 1,351 on gpu07; right comparator missing)
  • That long-soak memory is OK (eval + sharded ckpt cycles weren't in 47554; original real-run OOM researcher 05-12, reshT/reshF unknown)
  • That LL128 stacks — bs=128 reshT + LL128 (46030 C2) lost 6.4%, so LL128 on reshF is not a known-good knob
Right next experiment (Codex): paired same-node A/B — bs=128 reshT sel vs bs=128 reshF sel, 1000 iters each, sequential on the same node, no NCCL knobs, eval+ckpt off. If reshF beats reshT by ≥3–5% on median in both windows, the effect is real and LL128 stacking is the test after. If within ~2%, lock in "bs=128 parked." Also: ask the researcher whether the 05-12 OOM was reshT or reshF.

Open Decision Questions
  • Q1
    Does LL128 hold in longer runs?
    Stage A (300 iter) said +12.1%, CV 4.9%. Stage C (1000 iter, same config, same node) got +6–7% with CV 18%. Cause unresolved: cluster background load vs long-run in-process effects (GC, allocator drift, FSDP buffer state). Controlled 3× replicate of A0 vs A2 was proposed but not run.
  • Q2
    What caused the original bs=128 OOM?
    A 1000-iter memory profile (job 45282, two forced eval+checkpoint cycles) survived with 33 GB peak, no retries, no OOMs. But the researcher-reported real-run OOM was in actual production training — longer horizon, real data, possibly specific allocator state after many checkpoint cycles. Now that bs=128 + reshF + sel AC is back as a candidate (47554, 18.6 GiB peak, well under 80 GiB available), the OOM question matters again: was the original real-run OOM under reshT or reshF? Need to check. Either way, a longer real-eval-cycle validation must precede any production switch.
  • Q3
    When does run.sh get updated?
    After: (a) the bs=128 reshF + LL128 stacking question resolves (one job), (b) chosen production cell survives a longer validation run on a clean node (>1000 iters, repeated eval+checkpoint), and (c) the bs=128 OOM-history check completes. Candidates: bs=96 reshT sel (current safe default), bs=96 reshT sel + LL128, or bs=128 reshF sel (± LL128).
  • Q4
    Is NCCL the right growth lever at all?
    NCCL knobs can recover at most ~1–2 pp MFU. Current: ~7–8%. Target: 30%. The 22 pp gap likely requires gradient accumulation (fewer NCCL syncs per effective-batch step), coarser FSDP2 wrapping (fewer large collectives), or more nodes. Knob tuning is exhausted as the primary lever.
  • Q5
    Is FSDP2 costing absolute throughput vs DDP at bs=96?
    First read (2026-05-21): Job 48312 (DDP bs=96 noAC, gpu03, 1000 i) finished. iter 400–999: mean 1,387 img/s (median 1,379), MFU mean 7.94 %, step_ms 545, peak alloc 25.8 GB. Matched FSDP2 cell 45367 (bs=96 reshT noAC, gpu07): 1,279 img/s, 7.32 %, 600 ms, 24.9 GB. Observed Δ +108 img/s (+8.4 %), +0.62 pp MFU, +0.9 GB peak alloc. Eval+checkpoint at iter 400/800 did not exceed training peak. BUT: single run, cross-node + cross-date, with a monotonic within-run rise (1,310 → 1,373 → 1,419 img/s) that exceeds the reported Δ. Attribution deferred.

    Caveat about the archived DDP rows (added 2026-05-21): the archived 2026-04-03 DDP screening (9630/9631/9651) shows 18–23 % MFU and ~4 k img/s at bs=128, but those were 100-iter short soaks with the same MFU formula. The matched May 21 long-soak does not reproduce that throughput regime. Until the original 100-iter recipe is rerun on today's codebase, the archived DDP numbers are historical context, not a current target — do not use them as evidence FSDP2 is leaving 3× on the table. ⚠ Measurement caveat (added 2026-05-21). These DDP rows are archived pre-Phase-5 short-soak screening runs (100 iters total), not current performance targets. The new matched May 21 DDP bs=96 calibration (job 48312, 1000 iters, same MFU formula 226.4 GMACs/img, same dataset, same script knobs) reported steady-state ~1,400 img/s · ~8 % MFU on iter 400–999 — far below the archived ~4,042 img/s / 23 % MFU. Re-parsing 9631 shows step_ms held at ~250 ms across its full iter 10–99 window with no degradation; 48312 starts at iter 10 = 198 ms but degrades to ~545 ms by iter 30 and stays there. Two plausible reads: (a) 9631 was simply too short — it lacks a matched long-window validation and may have measured a startup regime the longer run leaves; (b) code/runtime drift across the 7 weeks since 2026-04-03 changed steady-state kernel timing. Hypothesis (a) and (b) are not mutually exclusive. Additional confounds we have not yet ruled out: recipe/config drift (precision, crops, masks, allocator, env), runtime drift (PyTorch/NCCL/CUDA, GPU clocks, thermal), compile/dynamic-shape recompiles after early iters (iBOT mask shapes vary by step), per-rank stragglers timed at rank 0. Until the original 100-iter DDP screening recipe is rerun on today's codebase, treat 18–23 % MFU as historical context only — not as evidence FSDP2 should match ~4 k img/s.
